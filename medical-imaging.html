Medical Imaging: Diabetic Retinopathy Screening

Python PyTorch OpenCV FastAPI Research software

A screening tool that grades retina photos for diabetic retinopathy, built to fail safely: bad photos and borderline cases go to a human instead of getting a confident wrong answer.

My role: a solo project. I built everything: the image quality and anatomy checks, the model and its training, calibration, the grading web app and the audit log.

    The images are synthetic retinas generated by the project (make_demo_data.py), not patient data. The gate results are real outputs of the project's code on these images, and the slider uses the exact thresholds from predictor.py. Research software, not a medical device.

    Design decisions, measured

    512 pxnot 224. A tiny lesion shrinks to 1.3 px at 224, indistinguishable from noise.
    142 / 400patients leaked into both train and test with a naive split. Splits are grouped by patient.
    0.102 → 0.031calibration error with isotonic. The popular choice (temperature) made it worse.
    Ordinalhead, so mistaking grade 4 for 0 costs far more than 3 for 4.

    Why it's built this way

    Ungradable is not negative. In real screening, 5–20% of photos are unusable. The quality gate runs first, and a blurred image is never quietly graded as "healthy".

    It can say "I'm not sure". Cases near the decision boundary fall in an abstention band and go to a human grader, and every grader verdict is logged to detect drift after deployment.

    Honest status. The full pipeline runs end to end on synthetic data. It has not been validated on real clinical data yet; external validation on the Messidor-2 dataset is the next step.
